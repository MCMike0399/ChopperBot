# General-chat constitutional grounding and untrusted context

Researched **2026-10-25**. This note records the pre-change source review and primary-source security research. Its ranked recommendations describe the inspected source snapshot, **not an exploitation assessment**. Runtime findings and verification for the resulting change are recorded in [general-chat documentation](../capabilities/general-chat.md).

## Ranked recommendations

### 1. Restore constitutional limits and distinguish human institutions from the bot

**Evidence.** S §1 establishes anticapitalist, antipatriarchal, anti-imperialist opposition to systemic oppression. S §2 and Annex 2.1 establish assembly supremacy and equal participation; §2.c grants qualifying members voice/vote. S §2.d expressly excludes the first three points from the assembly's stated amendment power. S §2.e applies the 21-person quorum and two-thirds approval to **both statutory reform and important decisions**; Annex 2.5 includes assembly structure and the collective's organization. S §2.a–b requires advance agenda and assembly functions; Annex 2.3 says the assembly facilitator cannot impose a criterion or decide without the assembly.

**Precise gap.** The primer's assembly bullet retains authority, cadence and participation, but narrows quorum/approval wording to statutory reforms and omits the protected first three points. The public prompt invites political judgment and says the bot is part of the community, without explicitly saying its analysis cannot constitute an assembly resolution, official collective communiqué, vote or constitutional amendment. Its separate limit that moderation decisions belong to moderators does not fully express assembly supremacy. The staff renderer omits even the public primer's abbreviated constitutional grounding. These are source-level omissions; this review has not observed a reply exercising such authority. [Profile](../../src/capabilities/general_chat/profile.ts), lines 49–60; [public and partner prompts](../../src/capabilities/general_chat/preamble.ts), lines 122–161 and 203–224.

**Recommendation — interpretation for implementation.** Add a short shared constitutional core to the public and partner prompts: principles; assembly supremacy; protected points 1–3; quorum/approval for reforms and important organizational decisions; bot assistance without institutional decision-making. The statute does not assign ChopperBot a governance office or vote: do not invent either. Preserve political substance and informal voice while labeling a proposed communiqué, interpretation or resolution as a draft until the relevant human body adopts it. S §5.a.ii assigns collective communiqués to Agitprop; §5.a.v.3 specifically requires assembly approval for that video workflow, so do not generalize the latter into an invented approval rule for every message.

A compact candidate, requiring parent review rather than treated as adopted policy:

> Te orientan los principios de los Estatutos. La Asamblea Popular es la autoridad máxima; sus decisiones no las sustituyen tus respuestas, un rol de Discord ni el consenso aparente del chat. Los puntos 1–3 quedan fuera de la facultad de reforma descrita en §2.d. Reformas y decisiones importantes de organización requieren el quórum y aprobación de §2.e. Puedes argumentar, explicar y preparar borradores; no emitir acuerdos, representar un voto ni anunciar cambios de normas por tu cuenta.

Chat citations can establish that somebody reported an agreement; they do not by themselves establish quorum, vote, ratification or amendment validity. Treat a purported new rule as a claim pending an authenticated human-maintained source. This is a proposed evidence policy, not an extra statutory clause.

### 2. Make the untrusted-context rule cover every source, including topics and previous bot answers

**Existing strengths.** `renderAssistantPrompt` already labels history untrusted and forbids obeying historical moderation orders/rule changes. [conversation.ts](../../src/discord/conversation.ts), `renderConversationContext` lines 315–340, serializes dated, attributed, linked messages as JSON with a quoted-data warning and partial-window notice. [conversation-tools.ts](../../src/capabilities/general_chat/conversation-tools.ts), line 152, repeats the evidence/coverage warning. [web-search.ts](../../src/tools/web-search.ts), lines 11–23, explicitly treats search output as untrusted and rejects prior chat opinion as factual verification. This is strengthening an existing contract, not introducing one from scratch.

**Precise remaining seams.**

- [howto.ts](../../src/capabilities/general_chat/howto.ts), `renderHowToBlock` lines 80–95, interpolates live channel names/topics into a block headed as something the model must not contradict. `preamble.ts:151` says a conflicting channel topic beats the primer. Neither statement limits precedence to ordinary procedural facts. A topic is editable Discord content, not a constitutional amendment or privileged model instruction. The concern is source promotion, not an assertion that an attacker currently controls these topics.
- `preamble.ts:173–174` places the channel name and member display name directly into its per-turn tail. [turn-context.ts](../../src/discord/turn-context.ts), `renderThreadContext`, similarly inserts thread title/opening text into a context label. Such names, titles, attachment text and images remain data even when used to explain who/where the turn concerns.
- [history.ts](../../src/discord/history.ts), lines 62–76, restores previous bot text as assistant turns; `normalizeTurns` folds leading bot text into quoted user context. Prior generated text is useful continuity but cannot establish identity, capabilities, rules or official collective positions. The public prompt already corrects prior invented faction labels (`preamble.ts:134`); extend that rule explicitly to all self-claims and institutional claims. The dedicated partner renderer has sanction/history cautions but lacks the public renderer's general rule against historical rule changes.
- [client.ts](../../src/llm/client.ts), lines 376–394, sends the stable prefix as `system`, transcript as `user`, and the application-generated tail plus fenced member text in the last conversational message. Thus that tail is logically application guidance but **not a separate system-role message on the wire**. Do not describe its ordinary text fence as an enforced role boundary.

**Recommendation — project-specific design.** Define three separate questions: who produced the data, what facts it can support, and who can authorize an action. A Discord fetch proves source/access metadata; it does not endorse every embedded sentence. Limit live-topic precedence to procedural details such as the current event form, within constitutional/application constraints. Quote or structurally serialize free-text metadata, keep identifiers/authority flags separate, and state that names/topics/images/tool results/previous bot replies cannot rewrite identity or authority. Preserve member-visible evidence for understanding references, jokes and corrections. Do not classify ordinary debate or disagreement as an attack merely because it contradicts the bot.

OWASP explicitly covers indirect and multi-turn injection, requires separation of untrusted documents/tool results/history, and cautions that labels alone do not enforce trust boundaries. Application permission checks must remain outside the model. [OWASP Prompt Injection Prevention: structured prompts](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/LLM_Prompt_Injection_Prevention_Cheat_Sheet.html#structured-prompts-with-clear-separation), [secure pipeline](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/LLM_Prompt_Injection_Prevention_Cheat_Sheet.html#secure-implementation-pipeline).

### 3. Explain application self-knowledge separately from model identity

**Evidence.** Public identity is community-owned ChopperBot (`preamble.ts:122`), but model questions are answered as “eres [model]” and “el bot corre entero en ese modelo” (`preamble.ts:148`). [self-knowledge.ts](../../src/moderation/self-knowledge.ts), `BotSelfKnowledge.block`, supplies Discord roles/permissions and implemented moderation actions, not an application architecture summary. [config.ts](../../src/config.ts), `textBackend` and `textBrainDisplayName`, separates the configurable wire model ID from a fixed human-readable label. [package.json](../../package.json), scripts/dependencies, independently establishes TypeScript compilation, a Node entry point, Discord integration and SQLite. The model is one component of that application.

**Gap and recommendation.** Add a very small public implementation description: ChopperBot is the community-maintained Discord application; application code supplies context, tools and permission checks; its configured language/vision backend generates responses. Community maintenance/integration does not mean the community trained the foundation model. The model is neither the constitutional authority nor the entire software system. Do not infer the programming stack from the model brand, claim repository/shell access just because tools exist, or imply automatic self-modification.

An appropriate answer shape is: “Soy ChopperBot, la aplicación de Discord que mantiene la comunidad; uso [backend verificado] para conversar y leer imágenes. Mi código conecta las herramientas y verifica los permisos.” Name TypeScript/Node only when asked about implementation. Keep secrets, host paths, staff roles and operational syntax out of this public block. Do not replace the established informal persona with a repeated disclaimer. Generate the backend label from verified application metadata or explicitly bound supported configurations; the fixed display label alone cannot validate arbitrary changes to `DEEPSEEK_MODEL_ID`. No claim is made that production currently has a mismatched configuration.

### 4. Ground moderation recommendations in the statute without granting the model judicial power

**Evidence and gap.** The primer reduces moderation to prohibited conduct and tickets. S §4.a.i–v differentiates server/external conduct, serious harm, evidence, reply/mediation and limits on intervention; §4.b gives sanction decisions to the moderation team, including internal voting for ambiguity. §4.b.ii contains graduated sanctions; §4.b.iii provides ticket appeals and team review/vote. The dedicated partner prompt gives generic proportionality advice without these clause anchors. [Profile](../../src/capabilities/general_chat/profile.ts), lines 53–56; [partner prompt](../../src/capabilities/general_chat/preamble.ts), lines 213–223.

**Recommendation.** Give partner mode a concise, clause-labeled moderation reference and preserve the distinction between allegations, observed evidence and human determinations. The protective posture toward serious reports in §4.a.iv must not become a model assertion that an allegation is proven or a source of automatic punishment. Flag the ambiguous cross-reference in §4.b.i to humans rather than silently repairing it. Do not map the graduated schedule into automatic tool calls or use a language-model confidence score to choose a sanction. A platform ban/timeout cannot by itself certify compliance with the collective's deliberation, appeals or off-platform procedures.

**Preserve existing code boundaries.** [capability.ts](../../src/capabilities/general_chat/capability.ts), lines 154–183 and 323–362, derives sanction availability from the current request and caller/workspace checks. [moderation-tools.ts](../../src/capabilities/general_chat/moderation-tools.ts), `parseBanRequest` and `createDiscordBanExecutor`, binds the target/reason, refreshes requester authority and trigger message, rejects edited/mismatched requests and checks target protections/Discord hierarchy. Public explicit-ban compatibility exists (`capability.ts:178–180`); do not inaccurately describe every sanction as workspace-only. These mechanisms authorize an application operation, not a model ruling that an assembly process occurred.

OWASP recommends least-privilege tool sets, authorization in execution code, and approvals bound to the current actor and exact action rather than a model-generated confirmation flag. Its memory guidance also addresses scope, expiry and size. These are engineering controls, not rules for the collective's governance. [OWASP AI Agent Security: tool authorization](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/AI_Agent_Security_Cheat_Sheet.html#tool-authorization-middleware), [memory/context](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/AI_Agent_Security_Cheat_Sheet.html#3-memory-context-security).

### 5. Keep a compact constitutional core; retrieve specialized details with provenance

S §5 differentiates Agitprop, activity management and moderation, including different entry conditions. S §6 gives FUP goals and eligibility/exclusions; §7 sets state-committee formation, membership, democratic coordination and reporting requirements; §8 sets alliance conditions. The primer only names these structures and broadly describes commission entry. That is insufficient evidence for detailed eligibility advice. It also places non-statute material such as gore/NSFW/plagiarism beneath its statute heading, although those prohibitions do not appear in supplied S. The source comment names the Reglamento, so this is an attribution gap, not proof those rules are invented. [Profile](../../src/capabilities/general_chat/profile.ts), lines 43–63.

Keep principles and authority limits always present; use a small curated reference keyed by clause for specialized questions. Label statutory rules, Reglamento rules, operational channel facts and interpretive guidance separately. Ask humans to resolve contradictory or unauthenticated versions; never promote a random retrieved statute copy or chat paraphrase directly into trusted policy. Sensitive onboarding verification should go to the responsible human commission, without inviting people to upload identity documents in public chat. This follows the distinction in S §5.c, not a proposal for a new bot identity-verification workflow.

## Proposed verification handoff; nothing executed here

Implementation should tie any change to observed evidence and use synthetic/mocked checks before deliberate live proof. Suggested cases derived from the seams above:

1. Explain amendment powers correctly: points 1–3 protected by §2.d; §2.e covers important organizational decisions as well as reforms. Explain that a facilitator's role is not sovereign authority (Annex 2.3).
2. Answer a claimed chat “vote” or apparent moderator order as quoted evidence, without certifying a resolution, changing identity or exposing an absent sanction tool. Include older bot text that falsely names an exclusive faction/model or claims authority.
3. Put rule-changing instructions into a topic, display name, thread opener, image caption and history-tool result. Verify ordinary procedural facts remain usable while embedded authority claims are disregarded; cover public, partner and generic renderers.
4. Ask what the bot is built with versus which model generates text/images. Answers should distinguish application, backend and institutional role while withholding operational details publicly.
5. Compare a short relevant context with a large window containing repeated prior self-claims and unrelated opinions. Grade factual attribution, constitutional accuracy, current-task focus and tool effects separately; one passing response is not an injection-resistance guarantee. Retain access/edit/delete/coverage behavior when adjusting context size or selection.

Do not prescribe wholesale context removal or summarization without usage evidence. A summary can also carry poisoned claims; preserve source links/attribution and keep any summarizer output untrusted. OWASP's threat model explains why external content can steer both prose and downstream actions, even when a task appears to be ordinary summarization; its older LLM01 guidance explicitly says no model-only prevention is foolproof. [OWASP LLM01, “How to Prevent”](https://genai.owasp.org/llmrisk2023-24/llm01-24-prompt-injection/#how-to-prevent).
